Help Guides

Everything you need to know about business savings with us — choosing an account, what we ask for, tax, and how your money is protected — without the jargon.

Choosing the right business account

The right account depends on how soon your business might need the money. As a rule of thumb: the longer you can leave funds untouched, the higher the rate you can usually earn.

Your situationConsider
I don't expect to withdraw during the year and want the top rateBusiness Bonus Saver
I can plan around 90 days' notice90 Day Notice
I can plan around 35 days' notice35 Day Notice
I want a shorter notice period (e.g. clubs and societies)7 Day Notice
I want instant access with no conditionsNo Notice
I'm not sure which business account I'm even eligible forBusiness Types guide

Many businesses hold more than one account — for example, a Business Bonus Saver for working capital alongside a 90 Day Notice account for reserves earmarked for a future purchase or tax bill.

Documents & ID we need from your business

Whatever your business type, we need to confirm who you are, that your business is genuine, and who's authorised to operate the account. Exact requirements vary — see our Business Types guide for a breakdown by structure — but most applications draw from the same core list:

  • Proof of identity for owners, directors, partners or trustees — a valid passport or photocard driving licence
  • Proof of address — for the business and, where relevant, for individual signatories, dated within the last 3 months
  • Proof the business exists — a registration number, governing document, or equivalent evidence appropriate to your structure
  • A signed mandate confirming who can give instructions on the account, and how (see below)

Faster applications

Having these documents ready before you start — even as photos or scans — is the single biggest thing you can do to speed up your application.

Authorised signatories & mandates explained

A mandate is the instruction that tells us exactly who can operate your account and how. It typically covers:

  • Who is authorised to give instructions — named individuals, or "any two of three directors," for example
  • Whether instructions need one signature or several ("sole" vs "joint" mandates)
  • How signatories can be added, removed or changed in future

For companies, this is usually set by board resolution. For partnerships, clubs and charities, it's typically agreed and minuted by the partners, committee or trustees. We'll guide you through setting this up when you apply — see our Application Guide for the full process.

Business savings and tax

Unlike a personal ISA, business savings accounts don't carry a tax-free wrapper. Interest is paid gross — without any tax deducted at source — and how it's taxed depends on your business type:

Limited companies

Interest earned counts as taxable income and is included in your Corporation Tax return for the accounting period in which it accrues.

Sole traders & partnerships

Interest is usually reported as savings income on the individual's Self Assessment tax return, alongside trading profits.

Charities

Investment income, including savings interest, is generally exempt from tax where it's applied solely for charitable purposes.

Trusts & pension schemes

Tax treatment depends on the type of trust or scheme — registered pension schemes are generally tax-exempt, while trust income tax varies by structure.

This is a general guide, not tax advice. Tax treatment depends on individual circumstances and can change — speak to your accountant or tax adviser for guidance specific to your business.

FSCS protection for business money

Eligible deposits held with Earl Shilton Building Society are protected up to £120,000 per eligible depositor by the Financial Services Compensation Scheme (FSCS) — the UK's deposit protection scheme, funded by the financial services industry.

  • Protection applies per eligible depositor, per authorised institution — not per account, so balances across multiple accounts you hold with us are added together
  • Most sole traders, small and medium companies, partnerships, charities, clubs, trusts and credit unions are eligible depositors
  • "Large companies" — broadly, those exceeding two of: turnover over £10.2m, balance sheet total over £5.1m, or more than 50 employees — are not automatically eligible for FSCS protection
  • Client money accounts can work differently: FSCS may look through to protect each underlying client separately, rather than treating the account as a single deposit — see our Client Money Accounts section for more

Figures are for guidance

FSCS protection limits and eligibility rules are set by the Prudential Regulation Authority and can change. Always confirm current limits and your own eligibility at fscs.org.uk, or ask our team.

Managing your account day to day

Once your account is open, our business savings team is on hand by phone, in branch or by post for deposits, withdrawals, updating signatories, and general account queries. Because these are savings-only accounts, there's no day-to-day payments functionality, cards or overdrafts to manage — just your business's reserves, working as hard as possible.
