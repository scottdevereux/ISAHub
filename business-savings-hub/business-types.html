Eligibility & documents

Business types we support

Whatever structure your organisation takes, there's a place for it here. Find your business type below to see who can apply, what we'll ask for, and anything specific to know about FSCS protection.

Sole Traders & Freelancers

A sole trader is a self-employed individual running a business in their own name or a trading name, with no legal separation between the person and the business.

Who can apply

  • UK resident, aged 18 or over
  • Trading as a self-employed individual or freelancer
  • Registered with HMRC for Self Assessment (recommended, not required to open)

Documents we'll ask for

  • Proof of ID (passport or driving licence)
  • Proof of address (dated within 3 months)
  • Evidence of trading — HMRC UTR, invoices, or a business bank statement

Because a sole trader isn't a separate legal entity, FSCS protection of £120,000 applies per person, and is aggregated with any personal accounts you hold with us.

Limited Companies (Ltd)

Private companies limited by shares or by guarantee, registered at Companies House, with their own legal identity separate from their owners.

Who can apply

  • Active companies registered in the UK
  • Not dormant, dissolved, or in the process of being struck off
  • At least one authorised director able to act as signatory

Documents we'll ask for

  • Certificate of Incorporation and Companies House number
  • Details of directors and persons with significant control (PSC)
  • Proof of ID for all directors and signatories
  • Board resolution or mandate authorising the account and signatories

A note on "large companies" and FSCS

Most limited companies are eligible depositors and protected up to £120,000. However, "large companies" — those exceeding two of: turnover over £10.2m, balance sheet total over £5.1m, or more than 50 employees — are not automatically eligible for FSCS protection under the scheme's rules.

Partnerships

Two or more people or entities trading together, either as an ordinary partnership or a Limited Liability Partnership (LLP) registered at Companies House.

Who can apply

  • Two or more partners trading together in the UK
  • LLPs registered with Companies House
  • A partnership agreement in place is recommended, though not always required

Documents we'll ask for

  • Partnership agreement, or LLP incorporation certificate
  • Proof of ID for all partners
  • Proof of address for the partnership
  • A signed mandate setting out who can operate the account

Ordinary partnerships are generally treated as the sum of the individual partners' protected shares, while LLPs are typically treated as a single eligible depositor. Ask our team if you'd like this explained for your specific partnership.

Charities & Non-Profits

Registered charities, Charitable Incorporated Organisations (CIOs), and other not-for-profit organisations pursuing a charitable purpose.

Who can apply

  • Registered with the Charity Commission, OSCR, or CCNI where applicable
  • Smaller charities below the registration threshold are still welcome
  • At least two trustees able to act as signatories

Documents we'll ask for

  • Charity registration number, where applicable
  • Governing document — trust deed, constitution or articles
  • List of current trustees and proof of ID for signatories
  • Trustee resolution authorising the account and mandate

Charities are eligible depositors, protected up to £120,000. Investment income is generally exempt from tax where it's used solely for charitable purposes — see our guide to business savings and tax.

Clubs & Societies

Unincorporated members' clubs, sports clubs and community societies, run by a committee of elected officers.

Who can apply

  • Clubs and societies with a written constitution or set of rules
  • Current officers — typically Chair, Treasurer and Secretary — clearly named

Documents we'll ask for

  • Club constitution or rules
  • Minutes confirming the current committee and treasurer
  • Proof of ID for signatories (usually at least two of the named officers)
  • Proof of address for the club, or for a named officer

Clubs and societies are eligible depositors, protected up to £120,000, and can appoint any two or more officers as authorised signatories on the mandate.

Trusts & Pension Schemes

Family and discretionary trusts, bare trusts, small self-administered pension schemes (SSAS) and other registered pension schemes, managed by named trustees.

Who can apply

  • Trusts and schemes with a valid trust deed or scheme rules
  • All trustees identifiable and able to provide ID
  • Registered pension schemes hold an HMRC pension scheme reference

Documents we'll ask for

  • Trust deed or pension scheme rules
  • Proof of ID for all trustees
  • HMRC scheme registration details, for SSAS and registered pension schemes
  • Trustee resolution confirming account signatories

Trusts and pension schemes are generally eligible depositors. Depending on the structure, FSCS protection can apply per beneficiary rather than per trust — our team can talk this through for more complex arrangements.

Credit Unions & Cooperatives

FCA-authorised credit unions and registered cooperative or community benefit societies, owned and run by their members.

Who can apply

  • Registered credit unions authorised by the FCA and PRA
  • Cooperative and community benefit societies on the Mutuals Register

Documents we'll ask for

  • Registration certificate and Mutuals Register or FCA reference number
  • Society or credit union rules
  • Proof of ID for board members acting as signatories
  • Board resolution authorising the account and mandate

Credit unions and cooperatives are eligible depositors, protected up to £120,000, in the same way as any other mutual organisation.

Client Money Accounts

Solicitors, letting and estate agents, insolvency practitioners and other regulated businesses that hold money on behalf of their clients in a designated client account.

Who can apply

  • Businesses required or choosing to hold client money separately from their own funds
  • Regulated or professional-body membership where applicable — e.g. SRA, CMP schemes

Documents we'll ask for

  • Evidence of regulatory authorisation or professional body membership
  • Confirmation of the client account designation required by your regulator
  • Proof of ID for account signatories
  • Mandate or board resolution authorising the account

A note on FSCS and client money

Protection for client money accounts can work differently to a standard business account: in many cases FSCS looks through to the underlying clients, so each client may be separately protected up to £120,000, subject to conditions and how the account is structured. Speak to our team for guidance specific to your arrangement.
