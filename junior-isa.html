How it works

A Junior ISA (JISA) is a long-term, tax-free savings account for anyone under 18 living in the UK. Parents or guardians open and manage the account as the "registered contact," but the money legally belongs to the child. Anyone — parents, grandparents, family friends — can pay in, up to the annual Junior ISA allowance.

Once the child turns 16, they can manage the account themselves (though it stays locked until 18). At 18, the Junior ISA automatically converts into an adult ISA, and they gain full access.

  • Market-leading variable rate on the whole balance
  • Anyone can contribute, up to the annual allowance
  • Funds are locked away until the child turns 18
  • Only one Junior Cash ISA per child at a time
  • Interest is entirely tax-free, with no impact on parents' own allowance

Good to know

Frequently asked about Junior ISAs

Who owns the money in a Junior ISA?
The child does — legally, from the moment it's deposited. The registered contact (usually a parent or guardian) manages the account until the child turns 16, but cannot withdraw funds for their own use.
Can grandparents pay into a Junior ISA?
Yes. Anyone can contribute to a child's Junior ISA, as long as the total paid in across the tax year doesn't exceed the annual Junior ISA allowance.
What happens when my child turns 18?
The Junior ISA automatically converts into an adult ISA in their name, and they gain full access to the funds and control of the account.
