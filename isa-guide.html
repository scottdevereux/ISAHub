Understanding ISAs

Everything you need to know about tax-free savings — allowances, account types and the rules that matter — without the jargon.

What is an ISA?

An Individual Savings Account (ISA) is a tax-free wrapper for your savings or investments. Any interest, income or growth earned inside an ISA is completely free of UK tax — you never need to declare it, and it doesn't count against your Personal Savings Allowance.

Your ISA allowance

Each tax year (6 April to 5 April), you get an ISA allowance — the maximum you can pay into ISAs. For 2026/27, the adult ISA allowance is £20,000, and the Junior ISA allowance is £9,000.

You can split your adult allowance across different ISA types in the same tax year — for example, some in an Easy Access ISA and some in a Fixed Rate ISA — as long as the total doesn't exceed £20,000. Unused allowance doesn't carry over to the next tax year.

Allowance figures are for guidance

ISA allowances are set by HM Treasury and can change. Always confirm current limits at gov.uk before making decisions based on these figures.

Types of ISA

Cash ISA

Works like a savings account, but tax-free. Includes Easy Access and Fixed Rate options. This is what we offer at Earl Shilton.

Stocks & Shares ISA

Invests your money in funds, shares or bonds. Higher potential growth, but your capital is at risk. We don't offer this account type.

Junior ISA

A tax-free account for under-18s, managed by a parent or guardian until the child turns 16, and accessed by them at 18.

Lifetime ISA

For over-18s under 40, saving toward a first home or retirement, with a 25% government bonus. We don't offer this account type.

At Earl Shilton, we focus exclusively on Cash ISAs — Easy Access, Fixed Rate and Junior — because we believe in doing a few things well rather than spreading thin.

Key ISA rules to know

  • You can only pay new money into one Cash ISA per tax year (though many providers, including us, offer "flexible" ISAs that let you withdraw and replace funds freely within the same tax year).
  • Transferring an ISA between providers doesn't count as opening a new one, and doesn't use up your allowance.
  • Withdrawing money from a non-flexible ISA and paying it back in counts as new money against your allowance.
  • You must be a UK resident (with limited exceptions, such as Crown servants) to open most ISAs.
  • You must be 18 or over to open an adult ISA; Junior ISAs are for under-18s only.
